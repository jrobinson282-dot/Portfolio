## Featured Projects

### 1. Motorbike Phone Mount: Iterating Under Real-World Constraints
* **The Challenge:** Designing a phone mount for a vibrating road bike or motorcycle sounds simple, but keeping a device secure against constant high-frequency vibration and wind shear is a tough mechanical problem. 
* **The Journey:** My team started with a standard concept, but our initial FDM 3D prints in PETG revealed massive weak points under clamping stress. Rather than guessing, we took it back to the screen: we ran FEA simulations in Ansys to pinpoint stress concentrations, reworked the geometry in SolidWorks, and transitioned our prototyping toward a more resilient material (Nylon) while analyzing aerodynamic forces via CFD, and finding natural frequencies in modal analysis. 
* **Current Status:** Preliminary report complete; currently moving from digital iteration to final physical manufacturing.

### 2. Warman Competition Robot: From CAD Screen to the Competition Floor
* **The Challenge:** Build an autonomous robot to strict rule constraints and spatial limits, competing against 100 teams at Monash University—and make sure every manufactured part fits together seamlessly on race day.
* **The Journey:** This project was all about closing the digital-to-physical gap. I modeled complex sub-assemblies in SolidWorks, but the real test came when tolerance issues arose during fabrication. By tightly integrating 3D-printed custom components with structural timber and metal brackets, our team rapidly troubleshot mechanical clearances. Writing the official technical drawings taught me how critical precise dimensioning is when handing designs over for physical build. The result? **5th place out of 100 teams.**

### 3. Plastic Bottle Shredder: Optimizing for Strength and Sustainability
* **The Challenge:** Design a mechanical plastic shredder capable of breaking down waste bottles to repurpose into 3D printer filament, ensuring the structural frame could handle high torque without catastrophic failure.
* **The Journey:** I started with analytical hand calculations to model expected operational loads and torque requirements. To verify safety margins, I ran Finite Element Analysis (FEA) in SolidWorks, which showed me exactly where stress was concentrating on the cutter shaft and housing. By strategically trimming material away from low-stress zones, I optimized the geometry to reduce weight and cost while keeping safety factors high. 
* **Reports & Files:** *[Machine Design Report](https://github.com/jrobinson282-dot/Portfolio/blob/f60df40e72bd45aa6f0aa2ca937f98695e492df9/Machine%20Design%20Report.docx.pdf)* | *[Drawing Appendix](https://github.com/jrobinson282-dot/Portfolio/blob/5da78d95ab1342115bd37fd503546346b6243031/Appendix%20C%20Drawings-305.pdf)*
